About

I've always been a creative at heart. Over the years I've worked in photography, digital art and design, where creativity meets technology. The thread through all of it is a love of craft: the making, and the slow process of something becoming what it's supposed to be.

After decades in high tech, I wanted to go back to the basics. Clay is about as basic as it gets: earth, water, fire and my hands. It started as a break from screens. Now it's the main thing.

I build biomorphic forms by hand. I start with what my hands already know, then let intuition lead, the way a jazz musician plays: skill first, so that when the moment opens up, you can follow it. Lately I'm drawn to repetition: many small marks, holes or units that together become one thing. It comes from how I see the world. I've spent half my life in Venezuela and half in California, surrounded by people from everywhere, and I tend to look at how small things add up, and how many different parts can make one harmonious whole. That's what I'm after in the studio: a way of working where my hands lead and the form tells me what it is.
